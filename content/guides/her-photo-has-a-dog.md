She's got a dog in two of her photos, and you already know what you're going to type: something about the dog. Good instinct — the pet is a gift, an easy, warm thing to open on. Bad execution, because every other match typed the same thing. "Cute dog" is the "hey" of pet photos: technically an opener, functionally a dead end.

The fix isn't avoiding the dog. It's opening on it like a person instead of a comment section. Here are six lines guys actually send, each rewritten into one that earns a reply.

**1.**

> ❌ cute dog!

Gets a "thanks 🙂" and nothing else. You handed her a compliment about her pet — she can close it in one tap. Give her a *bit* to run with instead:

> ✅ ok your dog is clearly the one with the personality in this profile and you know it

*Why it works:* it's playful, it's about *her* (mock-accusing her of being upstaged), and it begs a correction. Corrections are the easiest reply on earth.

**2.**

> ❌ aww what's his name?

A question, and the most-asked one at that. She's typed the dog's name forty times this month. Guess instead — the move from [guessing something about her instead of asking](/guides/guess-instead-of-asking):

> ✅ that dog is 100% named something human like Gary or Susan. I refuse to believe it's "Max"

*Why it works:* she has to tell you if you're right or wrong, and either way she's writing a sentence with a smile on it.

**3.**

> ❌ I love dogs too!

You've told her a fact about yourself that half the app shares. It goes nowhere. Aim it at *her* with a read on the dynamic:

> ✅ you strike me as the owner who swears she's the disciplinarian while that dog runs the entire household

*Why it works:* it's a cold read on her, not a résumé line about you — the same reason [complimenting her choices beats complimenting her looks](/guides/the-compliment-that-lands).

**4.**

> ❌ what breed is he?

Fine at a dog park, flat on an app. It's trivia, and trivia doesn't build a thread. Turn the photo into a scene:

> ✅ the photo of you two mid-hike is doing a lot of work. she looks thrilled, you look like you're regretting the incline

*Why it works:* you noticed a *specific* frame, not just "there's a dog," which is exactly how good openers come [from her photos, not her bio](/guides/open-from-her-photos).

**5.**

> ❌ he's adorable haha

"Haha" with nothing before it is filler. You've said the dog is cute twice now with extra steps. Add stakes:

> ✅ genuinely need to know if the dog approves of your dates or if I'm walking into a very high-stakes interview

*Why it works:* it's forward without being creepy — it assumes a date is on the table and makes the dog the gatekeeper. Light, cocky, easy to reply to.

**6.**

> ❌ dog people are the best people

A greeting-card platitude. It flatters a whole category, not her. Get specific and a little wrong on purpose:

> ✅ that is a dog that has definitely eaten one entire shoe of yours and you forgave it immediately. no notes, correct behavior

*Why it works:* the small, confident, slightly-off guess gives her something to confirm or fix — and "actually it was my whole couch" is a much better second message than "thanks."

---

The thread through all six: don't *report* that the dog is cute — she knows. Make a claim, guess a name, read the scene, raise the stakes. The dog is the doorway; the opener is what you do once you're through it.
