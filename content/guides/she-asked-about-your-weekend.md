"What are you up to this weekend?" is the single most-wasted message on dating apps.

Not because it's a bad question — because of what guys do with it. She asks, and he answers it like a form. Errands, gym, maybe seeing a friend. Accurate, complete, and utterly dead on arrival.

Here's the thing worth internalizing: she almost never actually cares about your Saturday. It's the question people ask when they want the conversation to move somewhere and don't want to be the one who says so. Sometimes it's a plain green light — she's checking whether you're free and whether you'll do anything about it. Sometimes it's just her looking for a topic with a pulse. Either way, an answer that's only information kills it.

So: six real answers, and the rewrite for each.

---

**Weak:** *"not much, you?"*

**Better:**
> genuinely nothing, which after last weekend is the plan. i helped a friend move a piano up three flights and my body has filed a complaint. you?

*Why:* "Not much" is technically the same content with the flavour boiled off. Same free weekend, one specific image attached, and the bounce-back now has something behind it. You don't need a better weekend — you need one detail from it.

---

**Weak:** *"just working, might see some friends"*

**Better:**
> working saturday, which is my own fault for saying "sure, i'll cover it" in a meeting i wasn't listening to. sunday i'm free and dangerously close to buying a bread maker. talk me out of it

*Why:* The original is two facts and no personality. The rewrite says the same thing, self-deprecates once without grovelling, and ends with something she can actually reply to. It also drops "sunday i'm free" without making it a request — which is how you signal availability without asking for anything.

---

**Weak:** *"nothing planned actually 😅"*

**Better:**
> nothing, and i've decided that's the point. current shortlist: the new place on Union, a long walk with a podcast, or lying face down until monday. open to being outvoted

*Why:* The apologetic emoji is doing damage the words aren't. An empty weekend isn't a confession — plenty of people are protecting theirs. Naming two or three real options makes "nothing planned" read as deliberate, and one of those options is now a place she can put herself in.

---

**Weak:** *"gym, groceries, the usual. wbu"*

**Better:**
> gym, groceries, and pretending i'll finally fix the shelf i've walked past for five months. thrilling stuff. what's the best thing on your weekend

*Why:* Boring weekends are fine. Boring *phrasing* isn't. "The usual" tells her nothing; the shelf tells her you're a person. And "what's the best thing on your weekend" beats "wbu" because it asks for the good part instead of the whole list — the same reason [asking a sharper question beats "how was your weekend?"](/guides/stop-asking-how-was-your-weekend).

---

**Weak:** *"I'm free! what did you have in mind?"*

**Better:**
> free from saturday afternoon. and i notice you asked me first, so i'm going to assume you have a plan and i'm just here to approve it

*Why:* The original is eager in the wrong direction — it hands her the entire job of inventing something. The rewrite gives a real window, flips the question back with a joke, and still keeps the door wide open. If she volleys anything back at all, that's your cue to stop circling and [put an actual plan on the table](/guides/lock-in-an-actual-date). Two exchanges of this, maximum, before someone names a day.

---

**Weak:** *"busy weekend! got a wedding sat and brunch sun 🙃"*

**Better:**
> wedding saturday, brunch sunday, so i'm booked in the least restful way possible. next weekend is completely open though — and i'd rather spend an hour of it with you than another six with my cousin's playlist

*Why:* A wall of plans with no follow-up reads as a soft no, whether you meant it that way or not. If you're genuinely busy, always attach the next opening. Busy plus a door is attractive; busy on its own just closes the thread and makes her wonder why she asked.

---

## What the rewrites share

Look at the six and the pattern is boring in a good way. Each one has **a concrete detail** instead of a category — a piano, a shelf, a cousin's playlist. Each one gives her **somewhere to stand**: a question, an opinion to have, a plan to disagree with. And each one is honest about availability without begging for it.

That's it. You're not being clever, you're being *answerable*.

When your own weekend genuinely is groceries and laundry and you're staring at the question with nothing funny in the tank, that's the moment [hintder](/) is for — screenshot the thread, pick a tone, and it drafts a few versions off what she actually said. You pick the one that sounds like you and send it yourself. First three are free.

One caveat on reading this too hard: her asking about your weekend is a decent sign, not a green light with a countdown. Some women ask because they're curious, some because it's the only small talk they've got. If she asks twice, or asks with a "free?" attached, she's telling you something — that's [one of the signals worth acting on](/guides/when-to-ask-her-out), and the correct response to it is a day and a place, not a better anecdote.

But the version that never works is the status report. Nobody has ever read "gym, groceries, the usual" and thought *I need to see this man on Saturday.*
