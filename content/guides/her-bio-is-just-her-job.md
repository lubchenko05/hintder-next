One word in the bio. *Nurse.* Two photos, no prompts, nothing else to work with.

So you open on the job — fine, that's the only door in the building. The problem is that everyone else opened on it too, with the same line, and she's read it forty times. A job-based opener isn't weak. A *generic* job-based opener is, because it proves you swiped without looking and then typed the first thing the word suggested.

Here's the fix, six times over. Left is what she gets all week. Right is what you send.

---

**Nurse**

> ❌ "so do you have crazy stories from work? 😅"

> ✅ "i'm going to assume you have seen something this month that would end me. not asking for the story — asking whether you were the one who had to explain it to the family afterwards"

**Why:** "crazy stories" asks her to perform, unpaid, for a stranger. The rewrite shows you know the job has a hard part, and asks about *her role in it* rather than a highlight reel. Way easier to answer honestly.

---

**Teacher**

> ❌ "wow teacher, that takes patience! what grade?"

> ✅ "teacher is a fun one because i can usually tell the grade from the profile. going to say Year 4 — old enough to be funny, young enough that they still like you"

**Why:** the first is a compliment plus admin. The second is [a guess instead of a question](/guides/guess-instead-of-asking) — she either confirms it or corrects you, and correcting a stranger is the lowest-effort reply there is. It also lands a small joke about her actual day.

---

**Flight attendant**

> ❌ "where's the best place you've been?? ✈️"

> ✅ "the thing i want to know isn't the best destination, it's the layover city you've somehow been to eleven times and have strong opinions about. what's yours"

**Why:** every man asks about the glamour. The job is mostly repetition, weird hotels and 4am alarms — asking about the unglamorous part signals you thought about it for more than a second. And "strong opinions" gives her permission to rant.

---

**Marketing / "something in tech"**

> ❌ "cool, what do you do exactly?"

> ✅ "marketing is the job description equivalent of 'it's complicated'. i'm going to guess you spend most of the week in meetings about a slide that gets deleted"

**Why:** she wrote something vague because the specifics are boring to explain. Asking her to explain them anyway is an interview. Teasing the vagueness is a joke you're both in on — same principle as [not turning the chat into a Q&A](/guides/stop-interviewing-her), and it's the exact move you want when [answering the same question yourself](/guides/answering-what-do-you-do).

---

**Lawyer**

> ❌ "remind me not to argue with you 😂"

> ✅ "the useful question for a lawyer is which flavour. corporate means you work until 9, criminal means you have better stories, family means you've given up on love entirely. defend your category"

**Why:** the joke about arguing is the single most-received message in her inbox. The rewrite is specific, a little cheeky, and hands her a frame to push back on — pushing back is engagement.

---

**PhD student / researcher**

> ❌ "wow smart! what's your thesis on?"

> ✅ "explain the PhD to me like i'm at a wedding and we've both had two drinks. i'll know it's good if it takes under thirty seconds"

**Why:** "what's your thesis on" makes her write a paragraph she's tired of writing. Giving her a constraint — a scene, a time limit — turns a chore into a game, and the answer is usually funnier than the real one.

---

The pattern is the same in all six: **don't ask about the job, ask about the part of the job she never gets asked about.** Everyone goes at the interesting surface. The reply lives one layer under it — the annoying bit, the repetitive bit, the bit she'd complain about to a friend.

And keep the job in its place. It's a door, not the destination; two or three messages in, you should be somewhere else entirely. If her bio is genuinely one word and nothing else, treat the photos as the real material — that's the whole point of [opening on a nearly empty profile](/guides/her-profile-is-basically-empty).

If you're staring at "nurse" and the only thing your brain offers is the crazy-stories line, that's a reasonable moment to use [hintder](/): screenshot the profile, pick a tone, and it drafts a few openers off what's actually there. You choose one and send it yourself. First three are free.

One job, one specific detail, one thing she can push back on. That's the whole opener.
