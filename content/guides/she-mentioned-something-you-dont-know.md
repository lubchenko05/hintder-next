She's typing about her weekend and she drops a name — a band, a sport, a thing at work — and you have absolutely no idea what it is.

There are three doors here and most guys pick one of the two bad ones. Door one: nod along. *"oh nice, love that."* Door two: open a tab, read for ninety seconds, come back sounding like a Wikipedia summary of a topic you learned about ninety seconds ago. Door three is the one nobody uses, and it's the only one that turns her reference into a conversation.

Here's what it looks like in three specific spots.

## "I'm seeing Fontaines D.C. in October"

**How to read it.** She didn't mention the gig to test your music taste. She mentioned it because she's excited about it, and excited people are looking for somewhere to put the excitement. The band name is the least important word in that sentence.

**The move.** Skip the band entirely. Go at the *feeling*, and ask the question only she can answer.

The fake-it version — *"oh sick, they're great"* — costs you the entire thread the moment she asks which album. And the homework version is worse than it feels: *"the Dublin post-punk band? their second record was huge, right?"* is a fact, not a reaction, and facts don't hand her anything back.

> *"no idea who they are, but you typed that with your whole chest. what's the one song I'd need to hear to get it?"*

That does three things at once: it's honest, it's clearly warm rather than dismissive, and it ends on a question she'll enjoy answering. Now she's sending you a song, which is a much better thread than you pretending to know a band.

## "I'm in fund accounting, it's as thrilling as it sounds"

**How to read it.** She's pre-apologising. She's had this conversation a hundred times and it always goes the same way — polite *"oh cool"*, subject change — so she's defusing it before you can. That self-deprecating tag on the end is the tell.

**The move.** Don't ask her to explain her job. *"what does that involve day to day?"* is exactly the interview question she's braced for, and it makes her write a paragraph of unpaid overtime. Ask about the human part instead: the annoying part, the strange part, the part with a story in it.

> *"I have genuinely no idea what that is. what's the bit of it that would surprise me?"*

Or, if the thread's already got some heat in it:

> *"you say that like you're not secretly the most powerful person in the building"*

Both work because neither requires you to understand fund accounting. You're not asking for a definition, you're asking for the good bit — which is the same principle behind [carrying a thread instead of running a Q&A](/guides/stop-interviewing-her). And if she does explain it properly, that's a gift: [her own words come back later](/guides/callback-her-earlier-text) as the easiest callback you'll get all week.

## "Mostly I just boulder now"

**How to read it.** A hobby she names unprompted is one she'd happily spend an hour on. It's also, quietly, a scheduling fact — this is where a chunk of her week goes. Worth knowing.

**The move.** Admit the gap, then put yourself in the picture rather than on the sidelines. There's a version of this reply that's genuinely fun and most guys walk past it.

> *"I know it involves walls and I know I'd be bad at it. what's the failure rate for a complete beginner, roughly, out of ten"*

She now gets to be the expert and rate you, which is a good dynamic to hand someone. It also sets up the obvious next step without you having to force it — a hobby she loves and you're comically unqualified for is a first date that writes itself. If the rest of the thread is warm, that's a clean [moment to name a day](/guides/when-to-ask-her-out) rather than keep circling.

## The rule underneath all three

Not knowing something isn't the problem. **Being boring about not knowing it** is the problem.

*"oh nice"* is boring. A Wikipedia paragraph is boring. "I don't know what that is, tell me the interesting part" is a man who is comfortable not being the expert — which, on an app full of guys performing competence at each other, is rarer than it should be. You also don't have to remember a fake opinion for the rest of the thread, which is its own kind of freedom.

Two things to avoid while you're at it. Don't apologise for the gap — *"sorry, I'm so uncultured lol"* makes her manage your feelings about a band. And don't make it your whole personality either; one honest *"no idea"* is charming, four in a row starts to read like you have no interests of your own. Trade something back: her gig, your thing, keep it moving.

If you're staring at a message with a word in it you've never seen and nothing good is coming, that's a decent moment for hintder — screenshot the thread and it reads what's actually on the screen and drafts a few replies in the tone you want, dry or curious or flirty. You pick one and send it yourself. First three are free.

Her reference wasn't a quiz. It was an opening, and "I don't know, tell me" is the correct password.
