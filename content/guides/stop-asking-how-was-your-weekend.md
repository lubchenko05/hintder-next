Some questions are threads killers not because they're wrong, but because they're *nothing*. "How was your weekend?" isn't offensive. It's just so generic that the only honest answer is "good, you?" — and now you're both stuck in the small-talk loop, waiting for the other person to make it interesting.

The fix isn't asking *more* questions. It's asking ones that hand her something specific to grab. Here are six defaults you're probably sending, each rewritten.

**Weak:**
> how was your weekend?

**Strong:**
> ok be honest — was your weekend a "productive, went to the farmers market" one or a "didn't leave the couch and regret nothing" one? no judgment, I need to know who I'm dealing with

*Why:* You gave her two lanes to pick from instead of a blank page. Choosing is easy; narrating a whole weekend from scratch is work.

**Weak:**
> what are you up to?

**Strong:**
> reading your profile it says you're "always cold" — so are you the person who brings a hoodie to a 75 degree restaurant or do you just suffer quietly

*Why:* "What are you up to?" asks her to report logistics. Calling back one line from her profile shows you actually read it and gives her a version of herself to confirm or deny — that's what a [good guess](/guides/guess-instead-of-asking) does.

**Weak:**
> how's your day going?

**Strong:**
> rate your day 1-10, and you're not allowed to say 7. 7 is a cop-out and we both know it

*Why:* A small, funny constraint turns a yes/no wellness check into a game. Now she has to defend a number, which is a conversation.

**Weak:**
> do you have any hobbies?

**Strong:**
> what's the thing you're weirdly good at that never comes up in normal conversation. everyone has one

*Why:* "Hobbies" makes her list yoga and reading like it's a resume. "Weirdly good at" gives her permission to be specific and a little proud — much better raw material to riff on.

**Weak:**
> tell me about yourself

**Strong:**
> I'm going to guess three things about you and you tell me how many I got right. one: you have strong opinions about a food most people don't care about. two: you've rewatched the same comfort show at least four times. three: you're the designated planner in your friend group

*Why:* "Tell me about yourself" is an interview prompt. Guessing does the work for her and turns her reply into a scorecard — she almost has to answer. If your first line already stalled, this is also a clean [way back into a cold thread](/guides/she-replied-just-haha).

**Weak:**
> so what do you do for fun?

**Strong:**
> what's your unremarkable-but-non-negotiable routine. mine is a specific coffee shop on saturday morning where the guy already knows my order and I'd genuinely be upset if it closed

*Why:* You went first with something small and real, which lowers the bar for her to do the same. Going first is underrated — it's the same reason [leading with a guess](/guides/answering-what-do-you-do) beats interrogating.

## The pattern under all six

Every rewrite does one of three things: gives her a choice instead of a blank, adds a small playful rule, or goes first so she can mirror you. None of them require you to be funnier or more interesting as a person — just to stop handing her a question that any human on earth would answer identically.

If you're staring at a thread and every follow-up you draft sounds like a customer-satisfaction survey, that's exactly the moment [hintder](/) earns its keep — screenshot the chat and it'll draft a few replies in the tone you want, so you pick the one that fits and send it yourself. First three are free.

One rule to leave with: before you hit send on a question, ask whether her answer could be word-for-word identical to what she'd send any other match. If yes, you haven't asked her anything yet.
