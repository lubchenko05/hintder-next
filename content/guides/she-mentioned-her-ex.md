Message nine. Going well. Then: *"ha, my ex used to drag me there every weekend."*

And something in your chest does a small, unhelpful thing.

Two bad moves follow, and most guys pick one. Either you go visibly weird — shorter replies, a joke with an edge on it, a sudden interest in changing the subject — or you go the other way and start investigating. How long were you together. When did it end. Are you over it.

Both come from the same wrong assumption: that her mentioning an ex is a signal about *you*. It almost never is. She's a grown woman; the last few years of her life contain a person. If the price of mentioning him is watching you get strange about it, she'll just stop mentioning things, and you'll have traded a real conversation for a polite one.

But the three ways it comes up are genuinely different, and they want different replies.

## 1. The logistics mention

> **Her:** i used to go every sunday, my ex lived round the corner

**How to read it:** it's a location fact. There's no emotional weight in it at all — she's thinking about the bagels, not about him. He's in the sentence the way a postcode is in the sentence.

**The move:** don't acknowledge the ex. Not coldly, not pointedly — just take the interesting half of what she said, which is the half about her. The guy who replies *"oh no worries about the ex thing haha"* has just made it a thing, out loud, on his own initiative.

> "every sunday is a genuine commitment. what were you ordering that was worth it"

That's it. You've demonstrated you're unbothered instead of announcing it, which are very different messages.

## 2. The venting mention

> **Her:** sorry i'm being weird tonight, just had a whole thing with my ex about the flat

**How to read it:** she's being real with you, which is a small compliment. It's also a fork in the road. One wrong turn is the interrogation — *"what happened?? are you okay??"* — and the other is therapist mode, a warm paragraph and three follow-up questions about the breakup. Both do the same damage: they park the rest of the night in his half of her life.

**The move:** acknowledge it in one clause, then hand her a door out.

> "flats bring out the worst in everyone, that's rough. what were you doing before it derailed your evening"

Ten words of warmth, then somewhere better to go. If she wants to keep talking about it she'll walk straight back through the door, and then you listen for a bit without competing — resist the urge to trade your own breakup story. Swapping wounds feels like bonding and reads like baggage. Mostly, though, she'll take the exit, because you've offered her the version of tonight where she doesn't have to be sad in front of a stranger.

## 3. The comparison

> **Her:** my ex never texted back either
>
> **Her:** you're not going to go quiet on me too, right?

**How to read it:** a temperature check, not a trap. She's telling you the exact thing she's braced for. It sits in the same family as the other [questions she asks to test you](/guides/questions-she-asks-to-test-you) — the point isn't the literal answer, it's how much wobble is in your voice when you give it.

**The move:** don't get defensive, don't over-promise, don't grovel. The tempting reply is earnest — *"I'm not like that, I always reply"* — and it's the weakest one available, because it puts you in a lineup with a man who isn't here and asks her to score you against him. Decline the comparison instead.

> "i refuse to be measured against a guy who can't operate a phone. hold me to a higher bar than that"

Warm, a bit cocky, and it steps you out of the lineup without arguing about it. Then change the subject — the fastest way to stop being compared to him is to be visibly doing something else.

## The rule under all three

Never ask a follow-up question about the ex. Not *how long*, not *what happened*, not *do you still speak*. You'll find out eventually, in person, when she decides to tell you. Every question you ask tonight buys information you can't use and hands him another ten minutes of airtime in a conversation that's meant to be establishing whether the two of you are any fun together. It's the same failure as [turning the thread into an interview](/guides/stop-interviewing-her), just with worse subject matter.

If you catch one of these and freeze — and the third one especially does freeze people — that's a reasonable moment to screenshot the thread and let [hintder](/) draft a few replies in the tone you want, dry or warm or light. You pick one and send it yourself. First three are free. It reads the actual exchange, which matters here, because the entire difference between mention one and mention three is in the wording.

Her ex existing is not information about you. Find the half of her message that isn't about him, and reply to that one.
