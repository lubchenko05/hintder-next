The hard part is supposed to be asking. You asked, she said yes, and then she typed four words that undid you: *what did you have in mind?*

Now you're staring at a keyboard trying to invent a bar. And whatever you send back in the next hour decides whether this becomes a date on a calendar or a thread that quietly dissolves over the weekend. She didn't ask because she's fussy. She asked because "we should get a drink" is a mood, not a plan, and one of you has to turn it into one.

Here's how that moment actually shows up, and what to do in each version.

---

**She says: "what did you have in mind?"**

**How to read it:** she's in. She's also checking whether you have an actual idea or whether you were just being social. The question is small and the stakes are low — she just wants to know it's real.

**The move:** name a *thing*, a *day*, and a *reason*. Three components, one message, no menu. The reason matters more than guys think: it's what makes the plan sound like yours instead of one you found by searching "date ideas near me."

> "there's a wine bar on Union that does a flight for the price of two glasses, which i find morally suspicious and take advantage of constantly. thursday after work?"

Notice what's missing. No "if you're free," no "let me know what works," no three options. One plan, one day, said like a person who has been outside before. She can move the day if Thursday's bad — that's a two-word reply and it still ends with a date booked.

---

**She says: "i'm easy, you pick!"**

**How to read it:** the single most misread message on this list. It reads like flexibility. It's a test of whether you'll actually decide. She's already said yes twice at this point and she's watching you hand the decision back like it's hot.

**The move:** decide, immediately, and don't perform gratitude for permission. The failure mode here isn't a bad choice — she can survive a mediocre cocktail. It's the follow-up question. "Cool, do you prefer wine or cocktails? Or food? Are you free weeknights?" That's four more messages before anything is real, and each one is a chance for her interest to cool.

> "dangerous thing to say to a man with opinions. saturday, 7, that little Thai place on Hill — the one with about nine tables and no website. if you hate coriander tell me now and i'll pick somewhere else"

The last clause does real work. It's a joke, but it's also a genuine out, so "decisive" never tips into "didn't consider me at all."

---

**She says: "sure, when are you free?"**

**How to read it:** she's flipped the logistics to you but skipped the *what*. Usually that means she's fine with anything reasonable and just wants a slot in a calendar. Sometimes it means she's slightly hedging — a coffee-sized commitment, not a dinner-sized one.

**The move:** answer with two windows, not your whole week. Handing over a full availability calendar makes it feel like a scheduling meeting, and a wide-open week reads like nothing else is happening in your life.

> "thursday or sunday afternoon both work. sunday's better if you want the version where we can stay for three hours"

You've narrowed it to a choice she can answer in one word, and you've quietly flagged that you'd rather have the longer one. Once she picks, [confirm it the day before](/guides/confirming-the-date-day-before) and don't renegotiate it in between.

---

## The three rules under all of it

**Cheap, short, and easy to leave.** A bar, a coffee, a walk with a specific destination. Not dinner, not a two-hour drive, not an event with tickets you've already bought. Low stakes get a yes faster because leaving early is possible — and the ones that go well run long anyway.

**Specific beats impressive.** "That place on Hill with nine tables" is better than the best-reviewed restaurant in the city, because it sounds like somewhere you go rather than somewhere you researched last night.

**A day beats a vibe.** "Sometime next week" is how a yes turns into nothing. Name the day. If she can't do it, she'll say so and name another one — which is exactly the outcome you wanted, arrived at faster. This is the same conversion problem as [turning a soft yes into a real plan](/guides/lock-in-an-actual-date), and it fails for the same reason: nobody named a Tuesday.
