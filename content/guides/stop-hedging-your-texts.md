Read the last message you sent her. Count the words that are doing nothing: *just*, *maybe*, *kind of*, *if you want*, *no worries if not*, *lol*. Now delete them and read what's left.

That's the message you meant to send. The rest was armour — small verbal apologies you attach so that if she doesn't reply, you were never really asking in the first place. It feels polite. It reads as a guy who's already decided this probably isn't going to work, and who has kindly pre-written her exit for her.

Here are six of them, before and after.

**1. The ask, apologised for in advance**

> Before: "hey so this might be totally random but if you're free at all next week maybe we could grab a drink? no worries if not!"

> After: "drink Thursday? there's a place near Borough I've been meaning to try."

Six qualifiers, gone. The tell is *no worries if not* — you handed her the no before she'd considered the yes. Say the thing, name a day, and let her answer it. If you're unsure whether the moment's even right, that's a separate question with [its own answer](/guides/when-to-ask-her-out).

**2. The number, hidden inside a maybe**

> Before: "this is probably a weird question but would it maybe be easier to text? only if you're comfortable obviously"

> After: "easier to text than argue with this app — what's your number?"

"Probably weird" makes it weird. Nothing about asking is unusual, so don't frame it as an imposition she's doing you a favour by tolerating. A [clean, one-line ask](/guides/asking-for-her-number) gets a yes or a no; a hedged one mostly gets a maybe, which is a no with better manners.

**3. The double-text with a self-own attached**

> Before: "sorry to bother you again lol I know you're busy!! just wondering if you saw my message"

> After: "still thinking about your Lisbon thing. did the sister actually make it to the wedding?"

*Sorry to bother* casts you as an interruption. *I know you're busy* answers for her. *Just wondering if you saw my message* makes the topic your unanswered message rather than anything she'd enjoy replying to. Send something with content in it instead — there's a [whole way to double-text](/guides/how-to-double-text-without-being-needy) that doesn't involve auditing her silence.

**4. The opinion, immediately withdrawn**

> Before: "not gonna lie I kind of think pineapple on pizza is fine haha but I mean each to their own"

> After: "pineapple on pizza is correct and I'll be taking no questions."

Half a stance is worse than no stance. The *haha* and the *each to their own* exist to make sure nothing you said can be disagreed with, which also means nothing you said can be *engaged* with. Flirting is friction. Give her something to push against.

**5. The plan you didn't want to be responsible for**

> Before: "we could maybe do drinks or food or literally anything you'd prefer, I'm super easy, whatever works!"

> After: "wine bar on Bedford Hill, Thursday 7:30. It's small and the snacks are unreasonable."

*Whatever works* isn't flexibility, it's admin she now has to do. Pick the place, name the time, and leave one clear door open — "unless you'd rather eat properly, in which case I've got a second idea." Vague enthusiasm is [exactly how a yes rots](/guides/lock-in-an-actual-date) before it becomes a date.

**6. The compliment, defused on the way out**

> Before: "you look really good in that photo haha sorry that's probably weird to say"

> After: "the photo on the boat — you look genuinely happy in that one. Where was it?"

Apologising for a compliment retracts it, and retracting it is what makes it creepy rather than the compliment itself. Say it once, specifically, and move the conversation forward in the same breath. The [compliments that land](/guides/the-compliment-that-lands) are about something she chose, not something she was born with.

## What's actually going on

Hedging isn't a vocabulary problem, it's an insurance policy. Every *just* and *no worries* is a small premium you pay so that a non-reply won't sting. The catch is that the premium is charged to the same account you're trying to build: she reads a guy who is negotiating with himself in front of her.

The fix is unglamorous. Write the message, then read it back and delete every word whose only job is to soften. What survives is usually shorter, warmer and considerably more like a person. When you genuinely can't see your own hedges — and nobody can, in their own drafts — screenshot the thread and let [hintder](/) draft the same reply in a few tones off what's on your screen. You pick the one that sounds like you and send it yourself. First three are free.

One caveat, because this gets overcorrected into rudeness: unhedged doesn't mean blunt. "Drink Thursday?" is direct. "You're coming out Thursday" is a guy performing decisiveness at someone. The difference is whether her no is allowed to be a normal answer — not whether you wrote it for her in advance.

Delete *just*. Delete *no worries if not*. Send the sentence underneath.
