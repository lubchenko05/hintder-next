Isaac was, by his own account, excellent at first dates. Calm. Easy. Never too eager, never checking his phone, never the one who seemed more invested. He had a whole system, and the system worked — right up until it didn't.

Because the dates were fine. All of them. Pleasant, two-hour, well-lit evenings that ended in a friendly hug and a text the next day that neither of them really meant. Somewhere around date eleven he realized he couldn't remember what any of them had actually talked about.

The problem wasn't chemistry. The problem was that nobody had met him yet. They'd met the version of him that had decided, in advance, not to want anything too obviously.

## The hour before Powell Street

Noor was different in one specific way: he was nervous about her. They'd been messaging for a week and a half — she was funny in a dry, unhurried way and she'd asked him a follow-up question about his job that nobody had ever bothered to ask. He caught himself checking for her replies. He hated that.

So the old plan kicked in. Be normal. Be breezy. Show up at 7 like a man with somewhere better to be.

An hour before, staring at her confirmation text, he did something he'd been carefully not doing for a year:

> "Small confession before I lose my nerve — I've been looking forward to this all week and I'm weirdly nervous about it."

He typed it, deleted it, typed it again, and sent it before he could talk himself out of it a third time. That last part is the whole story. The line isn't hard to think of — plenty of people get as far as drafting it. It's the sending. (It's also, honestly, the kind of line hintder will hand you when you're stuck; it still only works because a human decides to press send.)

## What honesty actually bought him

Her reply landed in under a minute, and by the time they sat down the evening had already stopped being an audition. Nobody had to perform composure, because composure had been taken off the table by text an hour earlier. They talked for five hours. The restaurant closed around them.

Isaac's read on it now is unsentimental: he wasn't being brave, he was being efficient. Every date before that one had spent its first ninety minutes on two people establishing they were relaxed. Admitting he was nervous skipped all of it.

The thing men are most afraid to say — *I care about this, and it's making me anxious* — turns out to be the fastest way to find out whether the other person cares too. Noor had been nervous since Tuesday. She just needed someone to go first.
