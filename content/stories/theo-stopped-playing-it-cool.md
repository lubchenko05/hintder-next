Theo had a rule, and he was weirdly proud of it. Never reply first. Never reply fast. Let a message sit for at least a couple of hours — three if he could stand it — so nobody would ever mistake him for someone who was *waiting*.

He thought it made him a catch. What it actually did was kill things slowly. He'd have a great exchange going, feel the spark, and then deliberately let it sit until the energy leaked out of it. By the time he "coolly" wrote back, she'd moved on to someone who'd just answered her.

He never connected the dots. He figured the matches fizzled because they weren't that into him. They fizzled because he made a hobby out of pretending he wasn't into them.

## Bianca, and the reply he almost delayed

Bianca opened with a hot take about the farmers market being overrated, and Theo laughed out loud at his phone. He had a genuinely good reply ready — something about the dogs — and his thumb hovered over the old instinct: *set it down, wait, don't seem eager.*

This time he didn't. He sent it right away, while he was still smiling. She fired back within a minute, and suddenly they were actually talking, fast and loose, the way a conversation goes when nobody's managing it.

So when the natural opening came, he didn't over-engineer it. He named the thing he actually did on Saturdays and asked her along:

> "Saturday regular. There's a coffee cart in the corner that's genuinely worth the trip. Come judge the vegetables with me this weekend?"

It wasn't a clever line. It was just honest, specific, and — for once — sent at the moment he meant it, instead of three hours later for effect. That was the whole difference. When Theo was stuck on how to phrase it without sounding like he was trying too hard, a hint helped him land on the words. He's the one who hit send, right then, before the old rule could talk him out of it.

## The market they never stopped going to

She came. She judged the vegetables. She grudgingly admitted the coffee was worth it, which he still brings up. That corner of the market became theirs — a standing Saturday thing that outlasted the vendors' original pastry lineup.

The lesson Theo took from it is almost embarrassingly simple. Playing it cool isn't restraint; it's just a slow way of telling someone they don't matter enough to answer. The people who felt like a catch weren't the ones who made him wait. They were the ones who wrote back like they were glad he'd texted.
