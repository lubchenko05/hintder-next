Arlo had a rule, and like most dating rules, he'd never once questioned where it came from.

The rule was: after a first date, you don't text first. You wait. If she's interested, she'll say so, and then you know. It felt like patience. It was actually a way of never being the one who cared more.

It worked exactly as designed. Three times in two years, a date went genuinely well and then dissolved into a silence neither person broke. He told himself those women weren't that into it. He had no evidence for that. He'd made sure not to collect any.

## The night the rule broke

The date with Imani was a Tuesday, which was already unusual for him. Two hours turned into four. Somewhere in there she told him about her grandmother's radio, a heavy wooden thing that mostly picked up static, and how the static was the part she actually missed.

On the streetcar home, the rule showed up right on time. *Wait. Let her go first. Don't hand her the win.* And for the first time it sounded less like strategy and more like fear wearing a nice coat.

So he did the thing that had never occurred to him. He didn't send a clever line, or a callback joke, or the deliberately casual *had fun tonight* that could mean anything. He named the specific thing he was still thinking about, and then he said the plain part out loud:

> "Still thinking about the thing you said about your grandmother's radio, that the static was the best part. Tonight was good."

She replied in under a minute. She'd been in her car for twenty of them, writing and deleting the same message.

## Specific beats casual

The reason it landed wasn't the timing alone. It was that he pointed at one real thing — not *you're great*, not *we should do this again*, but the radio, the static, the detail nobody else in her life would have caught two hours after hearing it. That's what tells someone you were actually there.

That's the kind of line hintder is good at finding, when the feeling is clear and the sentence isn't. It drafts it; you're still the one who has to hit send. Arlo hit send on a moving streetcar with his thumb hovering for about ten seconds first.

Two years on, the radio sits on a kitchen shelf in a shotgun apartment and still only gets one station. They leave it on anyway.

The lesson isn't *always text first*. It's that the rule keeping you safe from looking eager is also the rule keeping two people in separate parking lots, deleting the same message. Somebody has to be specific out loud. It might as well be you.
